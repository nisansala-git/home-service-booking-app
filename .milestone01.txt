1. Problem Statement and Justification for Selection
1.1 Problem Statement
People who need help around the house — a leaking tap, a wiring fault, a wardrobe that needs building —
usually end up relying on word of mouth, a social media group, or whichever provider they used last time. There
is no single place to check who is actually reliable, compare prices honestly, or know for certain that someone
will turn up when they say they will. Discovery today is fragmented across informal referrals and directories
such as Google Maps that list contact details but offer no integrated booking, verification, or accountability.
Our own conversations with real stakeholders backed this up directly. A homeowner we interviewed said the
biggest problem was not finding a plumber, but that
     ➢ "service providers try to do their work quickly but get less attention for the quality of the work" —
          Homeowner, Interview 1

On the other side, a carpenter we interviewed told us that customers often change the scope of a job halfway
through, which makes it difficult for him to plan his time or price the work fairly from the outset. Both
perspectives point at the same underlying gap: there is no shared platform that gives homeowners transparency
and gives service providers a fair, structured way to manage bookings, pricing, and expectations.
The proposed Home-Service Booking Application addresses this by connecting verified providers with
homeowners through a single platform that supports clear pricing, dependable booking confirmations, and
accountability on both sides.

1.2 Justification for Selection
     ✓ Two interviews, opposite sides of the same problem — the homeowner wanted clearer pricing and
       faster confirmation; the carpenter wanted a proper contract and a deposit system to protect against
       customers changing the scope of work mid-job.
     ✓ The same usability gaps kept surfacing independently — we asked a homeowner, a carpenter, and
       an app developer to try the reference platform, Profit.lk, and all three raised similar problems (thin
       reviews, unclear booking status), while the carpenter also hit a broken OTP step that stopped him
       registering through the mobile app entirely.
     ✓ A developer's perspective grounded the requirements in reality — rather than only end-user
       opinions, we got a technical read on what's realistic: real-time availability and secure payment aren't
       just feature requests, they're specific engineering requirements (encryption, PCI-DSS style payment
       handling, authentication) that shape the whole system.
     ✓ A genuinely wide stakeholder web — beyond the homeowner and the tradesperson, there are family
       members affected by a stranger being in the house, neighbor’s affected by noise and parking, and a
       compliance layer (licensing bodies, insurers, payment gateways) that a real version of this product
       would need to satisfy.
     ✓ A realistic scope for this milestone — small enough to interview real people about in the time
       available, but rich enough to support proper personas, journey mapping, and a full set of requirements.

2. Stakeholder Identification and Categorization
3|Page
We grouped the people affected by this problem into three tiers, based on how directly they interact with the
app versus how much they are simply affected by decisions made around it.

2.1 Primary Stakeholders (direct users)
    ➢ Homeowners / Residents — the people who need a plumber, electrician, cleaner, or carpenter, and
      who would open the app to book one.
    ➢ Service Providers — plumbers, electricians, cleaners, carpenters, and similar tradespeople who
      would use the app to find and manage jobs.
We interviewed one person from each side of this tier — a homeowner and a carpenter.

2.2 Secondary Stakeholders (indirectly affected)
    ➢ Family members of the homeowner, who are around the house when a stranger arrives to do the work.
    ➢ Neighbors, who deal with the noise, parked vehicles, and general disruption of a job happening next
      door.
    ➢ The service provider's own employees or assistants, who show up to help with larger jobs.
We did not interview anyone from this tier for this milestone — our reasoning for them is drawn from what the
two primary-stakeholder interviewees told us about the people around a job (for example, the carpenter
mentioned that his company's project manager handles bookings and coordination on his behalf). Interviewing
this tier directly is a target for the next round of research.

2.3 Tertiary Stakeholders (external influencers)
    ➢ App developers / platform owners — the people who would actually build and maintain the
      platform.
    ➢ Local government / regulatory bodies, for licensing and safety compliance.
    ➢ Insurance companies, for liability coverage when something goes wrong on a job.
    ➢ Payment gateway providers, who process the money changing hands.
We interviewed an app developer for this tier, which gave us a technical and feasibility angle that the other two
interviews could not provide on their own.

3. User Research Plan and Rationale for Chosen Technique(s)
3.1 Research Objectives
    ➢ O1: Understand what actually goes wrong for homeowners when they try to book a home service
      today.
    ➢ O2: Understand what a service provider needs from a platform to make it worth using over word-of-
      mouth.
    ➢ O3: Get a technical/feasibility perspective on what's realistic to build, and what should be prioritised
      from a UX and security standpoint.
    ➢ O4: Directly test the reference platform, Profit.lk, with people from different stakeholder roles to
      surface concrete usability problems rather than guessing at them.

3.2 Chosen Technique: Semi-Structured Interviews with a Live Website Task
We chose one-on-one semi-structured interviews over an anonymous questionnaire, for a few reasons:
    ➢ Depth over breadth. With a small number of participants realistically reachable in the time available
      for this milestone, interviews let us actually dig into an answer instead of collecting a shallow tick-
      box response. When the carpenter mentioned that customers change requirements mid-job, we could
      follow up on how he currently handles that — a questionnaire would not have given us that.

4|Page
    ➢ We could watch, not just ask. Each interview had a second part where the participant used the actual
      Profit.lk website in front of us, performing a real task. This surfaced a problem no self-report question
      would have caught: when the carpenter tried to register as a provider through the mobile app, the OTP
      never arrived, and he had to fall back to the website. That is an observed usability failure, not an
      opinion.
    ➢ Different roles, different value. Rather than interviewing three homeowners, we deliberately
      interviewed across stakeholder tiers — one homeowner, one service provider, and one app developer
      — so the findings cover the experience from the demand side, the supply side, and the build side.
    ➢ A deliberate trade-off for this stage. Interviews take longer per participant than a survey, which is
      exactly why we kept the sample small and rich rather than broad and shallow right now. A wider
      questionnaire is a sensible next step once we have concrete features to validate.
Each interview followed the same two-part structure: Part A covered ten background questions specific to the
participant's role, and Part B asked them to complete a short task on Profit.lk and reflect on the experience.

4. Research Instrument(s) Used
We used a semi-structured interview script tailored to each stakeholder role, each split into Part A
(background/context, 10 questions) and Part B (a live task on Profit.lk, 4–5 follow-up questions). The full scripts
and raw answers are included in Appendix A–C; a summary of what each part covered is below.

4.1 Homeowner Interview Script
Part A covered: which services they typically need, how often they hire help, how they currently find providers,
what factors they weigh before choosing one, past problems they've faced, prior booking experience, how much
they trust reviews, what information they expect before booking, preferred payment method, and what they'd
change about the current process.
Part B asked them to use Profit.lk to find and evaluate a plumbing service, then reflect on ease of navigation,
clarity of provider information, and the booking flow itself.

4.2 Service Provider (Carpenter) Interview Script
Part A covered: the type of carpentry work they do, years of experience, how they currently find customers,
what information they need before accepting a job, their biggest challenges, how they manage bookings,
common customer complaints, what would make their work easier to manage, how they get paid, and what
they'd change about the booking process.
Part B asked them to try registering and browsing Profit.lk as a carpenter promoting their services, then reflect
on how easy it was to be found, contacted, and understood by potential customers.

4.3 App Developer Interview Script
Part A covered: their development background, what they consider essential when building a booking app,
must-have features, the biggest technical challenges, how important UI/UX is, how user feedback should shape
the product, required security features, how to serve both customers and providers well, recommended
technologies, and what they'd change about existing home-service apps.
Part B asked them to evaluate Profit.lk from a usability and technical standpoint — first impressions of the UI,
task efficiency, usability issues, missing features, and what they'd prioritise in a redesign.

6. Data Analysis (Method and Findings)
6.1 Analysis Method: Thematic Analysis
We used thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke's six-phase approach) on the three interview transcripts: reading
through all the answers, coding recurring ideas, and grouping those codes into themes. Because the sample is
small — three interviews — we are not reporting percentages here. The value in this data is the overlap: three
very different people (a homeowner, a tradesperson, and a developer) independently raising the same concerns
from three different angles.

6.2 Key Findings
6|Page
Theme 1 — Pricing Is a Trust Problem, Not Just a Money Problem
The homeowner ranked budget as a top factor when choosing a provider and flagged unclear pricing as a
recurring frustration. The carpenter said
    ➢ "most of the customers are worrying about the budget" - Carpenter, Interview 2
and separately noted that customers changing requirements mid-job throws pricing off even further. This isn't
only about the amount of money involved — it's about whether either side can trust the number they agreed on.

Theme 2 — The Booking Confirmation Moment Is Weak
The homeowner told us she
    ➢ "expected to receive a booking confirmation immediately after submitting the request" - Homeowner,
        Interview 1

but did not get one. The carpenter and developer both independently flagged unclear or slow confirmation as a
gap in the current experience. This is one specific, fixable moment in the flow that is causing a disproportionate
amount of anxiety.

Theme 3 — A Broken Registration Step Nearly Lost a Provider Entirely
The carpenter tried to register on the Profit.lk app using his mobile number, but the OTP never arrived — he
had to abandon the app and switch to the website to continue. This is one of the clearest, most concrete usability
findings we collected: a technical failure at the very first step of onboarding a service provider, observed directly
rather than reported after the fact.

Theme 4 — Everyone Wants Real Reviews and Real Work History
All three interviewees, independently, raised the same gap: the homeowner wanted
    ➢ "more verified customer reviews and clearer pricing information" - Homeowner, Interview 1
the carpenter wanted
    ➢ "more details about previous work and customer reviews" - Carpenter, Interview 2
and the developer separately recommended a booking history page as a feature the site is missing. Reviews and
work history aren't a nice extra here — they're the main thing all three groups rely on to judge trust.

Theme 5 — Real-Time Availability and Notifications Came Up Everywhere
The homeowner wants live availability and faster confirmation; the carpenter wants notifications for new job
requests; the developer separately named real-time provider availability and push notifications among his top
recommendations. Three different roles arriving at the same feature — from the need side, the supply side, and
the build side — is a strong signal that these matters.

Theme 6 — Providers Need Structure the App Doesn't Currently Give Them
The carpenter's answers point at a problem specific to his side of the transaction: customers changing scope
mid-job. His own suggested fix was
    ➢ "making a good contract before starting the work, and taking a deposit before the work starts" -
        Carpenter, Interview 2

A well-designed screen will not fix this by itself - it needs a contract and deposit mechanism built into the
booking flow.

Theme 7 — The Technical Foundations Have to Support All of This
The developer's answers frame what's technically required underneath everything above. He named
    ➢ "managing real-time availability, handling booking conflicts, ensuring data security, and providing a
      fast user experience" - App Developer, Interview 3

7|Page
as the biggest challenges in building this kind of app, and separately called out secure login, encrypted user
data, and protected payment processing as non-negotiable requirements.

8. User Stories Derived from Research Findings
User Story 1: Upfront, Honest Pricing
Evidence from research:
    ●    The homeowner ranked budget as a top factor and flagged unclear pricing as a recurring problem.
    ●    The carpenter noted that customers changing scope mid-job makes pricing harder to hold to.
    As a homeowner, I want to see a clear, fixed price before I confirm a booking, so that I am not surprised
    by the final charge.

User Story 2: Instant, Reliable Booking Confirmation
Evidence from research:
    ●    The homeowner expected a booking confirmation immediately after submitting a request, and didn't
         get one.
    As a homeowner, I want an immediate confirmation once I submit a booking request, so that I know it
    actually went through.
10 | P a g e
User Story 3: A Registration Flow That Actually Works
Evidence from research:
    ●   The carpenter's OTP never arrived when he tried to register through the app, forcing him to switch to
        the website.
    As a service provider, I want a reliable registration process with a working verification step, so that I'm
    not blocked from joining the platform on day one.

User Story 4: Verified Reviews and Work History
Evidence from research:
    ●   The homeowner wanted more verified reviews and clearer pricing information.
    ●   The carpenter wanted more visible work history and customer reviews.
    ●   The developer recommended a booking history page.
As a homeowner, I want to see verified reviews and past work examples for a provider, so that I can judge
quality before letting them into my home.

User Story 5: Contracts and Deposits to Protect Both Sides
Evidence from research:
    ●   The carpenter suggested a proper contract and a deposit taken before work starts, to guard against
        scope changes mid-job.
As a service provider, I want to agree a simple contract and take a deposit before starting a job, so that I'm
protected if the customer changes the scope of work partway through.

User Story 6: Real-Time Availability and Notifications
Evidence from research:
    ●   The homeowner wants live availability.
    ●   The carpenter wants notifications for new job requests.
    ●   The developer recommended real-time provider availability and push notifications.
As a service provider, I want to receive real-time notifications for new job requests, so that I don't miss out on
work I could take.

User Story 7: A Secure, Well-Built Platform Underneath It All
Evidence from research:
    ●   The developer named real-time availability, booking conflicts, and data security as the hardest parts of
        building this kind of app.
    ●   He separately called for secure login, encrypted data, and protected payment processing.
As a platform stakeholder, I want secure login, encrypted data, and protected payment processing built in
from the start, so that the platform is trustworthy and doesn't fail under real usage.

10. How Might We (HMW) Statements
HMW Statement 1
Grounded in: Both the homeowner and the carpenter flagged pricing uncertainty, and the homeowner never
received a booking confirmation.
How might we give homeowners clear pricing and an instant confirmation the moment they book?
    ●      Mandatory upfront quotes or price ranges per service category
    ●      Immediate, automated booking confirmation with a visible status
    ●      Flag or cap price changes made after the provider arrives


HMW Statement 2
Grounded in: The carpenter's app registration failed at the OTP step, forcing him onto the website instead.
How might we make provider registration reliable enough that a broken verification step never turns
someone away?
    ●      A visible resend option and status indicator during OTP verification
    ●      A same-flow fallback to an alternative verification method
    ●      Clear, specific error messaging rather than a silent failure


HMW Statement 3
Grounded in: All three interviewees independently asked for stronger reviews and visible work history.
How might we help homeowners judge a provider's quality before they ever meet them?
    ●      Verified, timestamped customer reviews
    ●      A visible work-history / past-projects gallery on each provider profile

12 | P a g e
      ●   A simple, trustworthy rating system that's hard to game


HMW Statement 4
Grounded in: The carpenter's biggest operational problem was customers changing the scope of work after it
had started.
How might we protect service providers when a job's scope changes partway through?
      ●   A lightweight digital contract agreed before work begins
      ●   An upfront deposit mechanism
      ●   A simple, in-app way to log and agree scope changes mid-job


HMW Statement 5
Grounded in: The developer named real-time availability, booking conflicts, and data security as the hardest
technical problems to solve.
How might we build a platform that stays fast, available, and secure as real usage grows?
      ●   Real-time availability and conflict handling built into the booking engine from day one
      ●   Encrypted data and secure authentication as baseline, non-negotiable requirements
      ●   PCI-DSS-aligned payment processing with proper access controls
